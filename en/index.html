Who's behind it

Who is Umut?

I started out herding sheep in a village. Today I support IB networks in Latin America.

  1. The village

    I was born in a village in southeastern Turkey, into a farming family. I'm Kurdish. Until I was 16, I herded animals.

  2. Istanbul

    Then I moved to Istanbul to study and work. There was no money, so I didn't keep studying: I finished high school and that was it. I worked as a waiter, a pool lifeguard and a hospital secretary, and in shops, restaurants, tourism and health tourism.

  3. 2020 · The pandemic

    I discovered forex during the pandemic. I spent six months without work, without money and in the red. In July a friend called me: a forex company was looking for Spanish speakers, and I said yes on the spot, because otherwise I would have gone hungry.

  4. 3 years at a broker

    At the broker I worked in support, conversion and retention, and as retention team leader.

  5. 2023 · Barcelona

    Three years later, in March 2023, I moved to Barcelona. For the first two years I travelled around Europe non-stop.

  6. 2025 · IB Manager

    In 2025 I started as an IB Manager. Working with IBs, I saw they didn't know how to manage their community, so I began passing on my experience: how to talk to the first client, the types of client, how to retain them, discipline and much more.

I watched the IBs who worked with me grow their communities, and I also saw that in Latam there was no academy training IBs in this. I'm the person who founded the first IB academy, the first school for IBs in Latam, and who created the world's first Role Play for IBs.

In short: in this school I share with you experiences that were lived, tested and led to success.

1-on-1 practice

Role Play: practise with me, not with your clients.

What is a role play?

It's a simulation: I play the client and you play the IB. It's not a class or a chat — it's the real conversation, with its silences, its objections and its uncomfortable moments. The only difference is that here, getting it wrong doesn't cost you a client.

Why does it work?

You can read the perfect script ten times, but when a real lead says something you didn't expect, it all disappears. What you practice under pressure comes out on its own; what you only read, you improvise — and every improvised conversation is a lead going cold.

What a session looks like

  • We pick the scenario based on the stage you're in.
  • First I do it: you see how it's handled.
  • Now you: I'm the client, and I don't make it easy.
  • We stop, I correct two specific things and we run the same scene again.

The scenarios, one by one

Each scenario practices one stage of the academy. Here you see the situation; the technique is worked on in the session.

Practices Stage 1

The first contact

Open a conversation with someone who doesn't know you, diagnose in two questions and answer distrust without sounding like a salesperson.

I'll be this client
I saw your ad… but I've seen a lot of scams. Is this a scam?
Practices Stage 2

The lead who left you on read

Pick up a conversation that went quiet, without pressuring and without apologising for writing. When to insist, when to change the angle and when to let go.

I'll be this client
Ah, ok. Let me look at it and I'll let you know.

That was six days ago. Nothing since.

Practices Stage 3

The client who just deposited

The first three days decide whether a client stays. What to say on day 1, day 3 and day 7, without recommending trades and without disappearing.

I'll be this client
I've deposited. What do I do now?
Practices Stage 3

The client who lost

The conversation most IBs avoid: being there when your client loses, promising nothing and not disappearing.

I'll be this client
I lost almost everything I put in. This doesn't work.
Practices Stage 3

The client who's leaving

Recognize the churn signals and know what to say in the first 24 hours, before it's too late.

I'll be this client
ok

10 days without trading. One-word replies.

Practices Stage 3

Moving up a package

Make the value of the next level obvious without pressuring — and recognise when the client isn't ready yet.

I'll be this client
I'm fine like this. I don't need more.
Practices Stage 4

What you promised three weeks ago

Answer when a client reminds you of something you no longer remember: without making things up, without excuses, and setting up the process so it doesn't happen again.

I'll be this client
Hey, you were going to send me something, right?

You don't remember what. The client does.

Practices Stage 5

Recruiting a sub-IB

Spot the client who can become your partner and propose it without sounding like a pitch.

I'll be this client
Could I do what you do, too?

Frequently asked questions

How much time do I need?

Every stage applies the same day you read it. The daily minimums —prospecting, follow-up and closing— add up to about an hour; with the live sessions and the weekly review, a bit more. If you have less, we adjust the daily number, not the method.

Will you teach me to trade or give me signals?

No. This doesn't teach trading and doesn't give signals: it teaches the IB's job — attract, support, retain and systemise.

What happens in the 30-minute meeting?

We talk about your community, your numbers and what's holding you back. It's not a sales call: there's nothing to buy. If it's a fit, you leave with access; if not, you leave with two or three concrete things to apply anyway.

What if I don't have a community yet?

You can join. Stage 1 starts exactly there: where your first clients come from and what to say to them. What you do need is to be willing to do the daily work.

Can I share the content?

Access is personal. You can use all of it with your community; what you can't do is publish it or resell it.
